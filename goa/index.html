The walkthrough

From a news item to a proposal on the client's screen.

Eight steps. Each one is live in the demo; every screenshot below is from the real site.

01

Signals

Radar collects public, dated reasons to call: openings, events, approvals, school holidays. Every item links to its source.

Why it matters: Reps stop hunting; they start the day with a list of fresh, verifiable triggers.

02

Score

Each trigger gets a transparent 0–100 priority score: certainty, distance to goa boards, who would buy, timing, source strength.

Why it matters: The best calls go first, and anyone can see why (no black box).

03

Match to goa boards

Every trigger is matched to the nearest goa sites using goa's public site list and real coordinates.

Why it matters: The pitch is about a specific board, not 'billboards' in general.

Billboards in 2033

Glasses are starting to put a screen in front of people's eyes. That doesn't replace great physical locations. It makes them more valuable, if you own the anchor and the data.

NOW · what's real today

Meta Ray-Ban DisplayDisplay glasses with a neural wristband. US from 30 Sep 2025 (US$799); UK and Canada from 23 Sep 2026; France, Italy, Germany from 13 Oct 2026. Not yet sold in Australia. [1][2][3]
Apple Vision Pro (M5)Spatial computer from US$3,499; in Australian Apple Stores since 22 Oct 2025. [4]
Snap SpecsConsumer AR glasses, US$2,195; preorders from June 2026, shipping this northern autumn in the US, UK and France. [5]
Android XR glassesGoogle and Samsung audio glasses arriving autumn 2026 (Samsung reportedly November), with display glasses to follow. [6][7]
OOH is growing, and going digitalAustralian OOH net media revenue reached $1,449.5m in 2025, up 11.43%; digital was 76.6% of it. MOVE, the shared measurement currency, gets its next iteration in 2026. [8][9]
Anchoring is solved techVisual positioning systems already locate a device to centimetres and keep virtual content pinned to real places. [10]

SCENARIOS · Duane's foresight, not a prediction

  1. NOW
    Screens on faces, early adopters

    Display glasses ship in a handful of markets. Digital OOH already lets creative change by time, weather and event. Radar starts the data layer: who is opening, what is on, where.

  2. ~2028
    Boards get a digital twin

    Each premium site has a precise, scanned anchor. A passer-by can opt in to extend a real board: menu, booking, directions. Measurement adds verified glances to traffic counts.

  3. ~2030
    The pushback arrives

    People reject ads that float wherever they look. Platforms and regulators limit virtual-only advertising. Content tied to a real, licensed, physical board is the trusted exception.

  4. ~2033
    Physical boards anchor the spatial web

    The premium physical location plus its data layer is the asset: the board everyone sees, plus an opt-in layer for those who want more. Owners of the best sites and the best local data set the terms.

The tension

Not everyone wants ads in their vision. That's the point: in a world of blockable virtual clutter, a real board in a great location is the one thing you can't swipe away. goa's boards become the anchor points of the spatial web, and Radar is the first step of the data layer that makes them smarter.
